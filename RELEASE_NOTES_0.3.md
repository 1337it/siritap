Button mode: get around your Mac the Apple TV way, plus a batch of fixes for clicks and taps.

**Website:** https://siritap.leet.ae

**Download:** `Siritap-0.3.dmg` below. Open it and drag Siritap into Applications, replacing the old copy. Signed and notarized by Apple. macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon and Intel.

### New: Button mode
Off by default. Turn it on in Settings → Pointer → Button mode.
- The pointer settles on buttons, links, text fields, files and Dock icons, with a thin outline around the one in focus.
- Swipe straight across or up and down to step to the next one. A longer swipe goes further, and a hard flick keeps going.
- **Automatic:** Siritap reads your intent from the swipe. A diagonal movement moves the pointer instead, and so does pushing past the last button in a window.
- **Hold the click to switch:** turn it on and off yourself, and swipe across the whole screen, from the Dock to the menu bar.
- Tap an edge to jump to the next button that way; four "Next Button" actions can be put on any button.

### Improved
- **Clicking the 2nd-generation remote now works.** Its clickpad was only partly recognised, so many presses did nothing. A press anywhere on the pad is now a click, and one press is one click.
- **Clicks land where you aimed.** Pressing nudges your fingertip; the pointer is put back to where it was before the press, and stays put while the click is held unless you slide to drag.
- **Fast arrow taps all register.** Tapping quickly used to drop about one tap in four. Taps on the 2nd-generation remote also fire sooner.
- **Hold still when pressing hard:** a new, optional pressure limit on the Pointer page.
- The live view of the remote stays at the top of each settings page while you scroll.

### After updating
macOS may ask for Accessibility again. If the remote does nothing, switch Siritap off and on in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility.

### Known limits
- Button mode needs apps that describe their buttons to macOS Accessibility. Most Mac apps do; a few don't, and there the pointer simply moves freely.
- 1st-generation remotes use the Mac's microphone for voice.
